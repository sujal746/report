## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 The question we are answering

> Can 4–5 coordinated shillers, each sending 5 good personal messages per day, bring us verified depositors and repeat bettors from competitor communities and Korean-language communities within 14–21 days — without spending much money upfront and without spamming anyone?

### 1.2 Short answer

**Yes, it is worth testing — but we should be realistic about what the test can prove.**

Here is the honest maths, using 5 shillers over 14 days:

- 5 shillers × 5 messages × 12 full outreach days = **310 unique people contacted.** [PROPOSAL]
- At realistic response rates, that produces roughly **0 to 5 verified first-time depositors** over the whole 14 days. [ASSUMPTION — see section 9.4]

That is a small number. It is enough to tell us **which messages and which audiences get replies**, and it gives us a first signal on deposits. It is **not** enough to prove that deposits will scale.

So we recommend treating this as a **learning pilot**, not a volume acquisition channel. The real goal for 14 days is:

1. Find out which audience replies and clicks at all.
2. Find out whether Korean-language outreach works better or worse than competitor-community outreach.
3. Build a tracking system that can prove where every depositor came from.
4. Build a small, trained team we can scale later if the signal is good.

### 1.3 What we are asking management to approve

| # | Approval needed | Why it blocks us |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commission / incentive terms (revenue share %, bonus per verified depositor, payment timing) | We cannot hire shillers without this |
| 2 | Working affiliate / referral links with per-shiller tracking | No tracking = no attribution = no decisions |
| 3 | Access to deposit reporting so we can verify first-time deposits | Deposits are the only metric that matters |
| 4 | Incentive budget ceiling (even if the plan is "zero cash", we need a cap) | Protects us from overspending |
| 5 | Legal / compliance sign-off for Korean-language outreach | Korean online-gambling rules are strict — see section 10.6 |
| 6 | Approved product wording for scripts | Shillers must not overstate or mislead |

### 1.4 The three priorities, in strict order

| Priority | Audience | Share of outreach | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Competitor platform users (Polymarket and other prediction-market / sports-betting communities where promotion is allowed) | **~64%** | They already understand prediction markets. No education needed. Fastest path to a first bet. |
| **2** | Korean-language sports / esports / prediction communities | **~24%** | Large sports-mad audience, but we must test whether they convert and whether it is legally sensible. |
| **3** | Existing Kixly users (inactive, deposited-but-never-bet, referrals) | **~12%** | Not cold outreach. Cheapest wins. Handled by the retention/referral workflow. |

### 1.5 Timeline at a glance

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Hire, onboard, set up links and tracker. No outreach. |
| Day 2 | Soft launch: 2 messages per shiller. Test tracking. |

## 2. Objective and Key Hypotheses

### 2.1 Objective

Acquire **genuine, relevant bettors** — people who understand sports prediction and might actually use Kixly — at minimal upfront cost, and prove that the channel works before spending money on it.

[PROPOSAL] Primary success metric: **verified first-time depositors who go on to place at least one bet.**

[PROPOSAL] Secondary metrics: reply rate, click rate, signup rate, first-bet rate, repeat activity, cost per verified depositor.

### 2.2 The five hypotheses we are testing

A hypothesis is just a "guess we want to check". We are testing five.

| # | Hypothesis | How we check it | What would prove it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | People in prediction-market communities (e.g. Polymarket sports-market discussions) reply to a polite, personal message at a usable rate | Reply rate on Priority 1 contacts | Reply rate under 5% after 150+ contacts |
| H2 | Prediction-market users convert to depositors faster than general sports fans, because they already understand "buy a position at a price" | Compare Priority 1 vs Priority 2 click → signup → deposit | Priority 2 converts the same or better |
| H3 | Korean-language outreach works if messages are written naturally in Korean, not translated word-for-word | Compare Korean arm vs English arm on reply and click | Korean replies are much lower or objections are cultural/legal |
| H4 | Community admins and micro-influencers produce more qualified users per hour of effort than 1-to-1 DMs | Compare admin-partner track vs shiller DM track | Admin track produces no verified depositors by Day 21 |
| H5 | A tiny team (4–5 people) can run clean, non-spammy outreach that communities tolerate | Ban count, complaint count, QA scores | Any ban or formal complaint in the first 7 days |

### 2.3 What we are *not* doing

Writing this down so nobody is confused later:

- ❌ No scraping of private member lists.
- ❌ No mass unsolicited bulk messaging or bots.
- ❌ No fake identities, fake accounts or fake "I'm just a normal user" stories.
- ❌ No fabricated testimonials, screenshots or earnings claims.
- ❌ No guaranteed-profit or "sure win" language.
- ❌ No contacting anyone who already said no.
- ❌ No contacting minors or people in restricted regions.
- ❌ No paying shillers per message sent.

## 3. Target Audience and Acquisition Channels

### 3.1 Priority 1 — Competitor Platform Users (High Priority, ~64% of outreach)

**Who they are:** People who already use or discuss competitor prediction markets and sports/esports betting platforms. They already know what odds, positions and settlement mean.

**Why they matter most:** We do not have to teach them the concept. The only job is to explain why Kixly is worth trying. That is a much shorter conversation.

**How to find them — practical research methods:**

| Where to look | How to search | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Public Discord servers | Use a public Discord directory (e.g. Disboard) and search topic keywords | Servers with active sports-market or prediction channels, and a channel where members actually talk |
| Telegram groups | Use public Telegram directories/search (e.g. TGStat) and topic keywords | Active groups, recent messages, visible admin/mod names |
| X / Twitter | Search keywords and hashtags; look at who replies to prediction-market accounts | People posting their own positions or match analysis |
| Reddit & public forums | Search sub-forums where sports betting and prediction markets are discussed | Users asking questions (a question = a conversation opener) |
| Polymarket & similar public discussions | Look at public comment/discussion areas on sports markets | People explaining their reasoning — these are the qualified ones |
| YouTube / Twitch chat | Channels covering sports analysis or betting | Viewers who comment with their own picks |

**Keyword examples (English):** "sports prediction market", "esports betting discord", "polymarket sports", "match odds discussion", "KBO betting", "sports trading community", "prediction market telegram".

> **Rule:** We are **not naming any specific community in this report as a confirmed target or partner.** Every community must be checked manually before we contact anyone. See the checklist below.

**Community check before any outreach — 8 questions:**

1. Does the community allow promotional DMs or partnership posts? Where is it written?
2. Is there a dedicated promo/partnership channel or a named contact?
3. Is the group actually active (recent messages in the last 48 hours)?
4. Are recent posts about sports markets, or is it dead / full of spam?
5. How many members post, versus how many lurk?
6. Is there a language split (mostly English, mostly Korean, mixed)?
7. Who is the admin/mod, and is their contact public?
8. Is there an existing rule against crypto or betting promotion?

If the answer to (1) is "no" or "unclear", **we do not DM there.** We contact the admin instead (see section 12).

**How to tell if a person is genuinely qualified:**

| Signal | Qualified? |
|---|---|
| Posted their own prediction with reasoning in the last 2 weeks | ✅ Strong |
| Asked a question about odds, settlement or payouts | ✅ Strong |
| Complained about a competitor's fees, limits or market selection | ✅ Strong |
| Only reacts with emojis, never posts | ⚠️ Weak |
| Only posts memes / off-topic | ❌ Skip |
| Is a bot or a promo account | ❌ Skip |
| Posted about responsible gambling or taking a break | ❌ Skip — do not contact |

## 4. Shiller Recruitment and Team Structure

### 4.1 What a "shiller" means in this plan

In this document, a **shiller** means an **outreach agent**: a real person who finds qualified prospects, starts genuine conversations, and introduces Kixly honestly. Not a bot, not a spam account.

### 4.2 Who does the work

| Role | People | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **BD Lead** | 1 (internal) | Runs the team, writes scripts, checks quality, owns the tracker, reports to management |
| **Shillers / outreach agents** | 4–5 | 5 quality initial messages per day each, plus follow-ups and logging |
| **Korean-language support** | 1 (can be one of the shillers) | Writes and reviews all Korean scripts, handles Korean replies |
| **Community partnership / affiliate manager** | 1 (optional; can be the BD Lead or shiller S5) | Talks to admins, streamers and micro-influencers |

### 4.3 Team allocation (5-shiller version)

We do **not** split the audience equally. Competitor users come first.

| Shiller | Main focus | Daily messages | 14-day total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Priority 1 — Polymarket / prediction-market communities (EN) | 5 | 62 |
| **S2** | Priority 1 — competitor sports/esports betting communities (EN) | 5 | 62 |
| **S3** | Priority 1 — mixed competitor communities (EN) + Korean backup | 4 EN + 1 KO | 62 |
| **S4** | Priority 2 — Korean-language communities (KO) | 5 KO | 62 |
| **S5** | Priority 3 — existing users + admin/partner support + Priority 1 overflow | 2 P1 + 3 P3 | 62 |
| **Total** | | **25/day** | **310** |

Segment totals: **Priority 1 = 199 (~64%) · Priority 2 = 74 (~24%) · Priority 3 = 37 (~12%)**

**4-shiller version:** drop S5, move the Priority 3 work (2/day) to the BD Lead, and reduce Priority 1 overflow. Result: 20 messages/day, **248 over 14 days** (P1 ≈ 149, P2 ≈ 74, P3 ≈ 25).

> ⚠️ **Known weakness to flag now:** the Korean arm gets only about 74 contacts in 14 days. That is a small sample. If Korean results look promising but unclear at Day 14, that is a valid reason to extend — but only for the Korean arm.

### 4.4 How to recruit shillers

**Where to look:**

| Source | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Crypto / betting Telegram & Discord communities | Post a clear, honest job ad where self-promo or jobs are allowed | $0 |
| Freelance platforms | Post a short, fixed-scope outreach project | Low |
| Our own active users | Ask users who are already active in chats | $0 |
| Referrals from the team | Ask existing staff and partners | $0 |
| University / sports-forum moderators | Part-time, sports-mad, trustworthy | Low |

**Job ad wording must be honest.** Example: *"Outreach agent for a sports prediction-market platform. You will message people in public communities, start real conversations, and log everything in a shared sheet. No spam, no bots, no fake accounts. Pay: revenue share + bonus per verified depositor."*

### 4.5 Skills we assess before hiring

| Skill | How we test it | Minimum bar |
|---|---|---|
| Written English | Ask for a short sample message they wrote | Clear, no obvious errors |
| Korean (for S3/S4 roles) | Ask them to write and rewrite a short Korean message | Must be reviewed by a fluent speaker; not machine-translated |
| Understanding of sports betting / prediction markets | Ask them to explain "odds" and "position price" in simple words | Must get it basically right |
| Community manners | Ask "what would you do if an admin says no?" | Must answer "stop and log it" |
| Honesty under pressure | Ask "what if the target is 5 messages and you only found 2 good prospects?" | Must say "send 2, log why" |
| Reliability | Check they can log daily for a 3-day paid trial | 100% of daily logs submitted on time |

### 4.6 Onboarding (Day 1, about 3 hours)

1. **Briefing (45 min):** what Kixly is, what we are testing, what is forbidden.
2. **Product walkthrough (30 min):** how to sign up, deposit, place a bet, withdraw. Every shiller must do this themselves with a tiny amount so they can answer basic questions. [PROPOSAL — needs approval for test funds]
3. **Rules session (30 min):** community rules, disclosure rules, no-spam rules, stop rules.
4. **Tracker training (30 min):** how to claim a prospect, how to log, how to fill the daily report.
5. **Practice (45 min):** each shiller writes 5 messages. BD Lead reviews and approves or rejects each one.
6. **Sign-off:** shiller signs the outreach & disclosure guidelines (section 11, item 10).

### 4.7 High-quality DM vs spam — the checklist

**A message is high quality only if it passes all 8:**

1. ✅ You read something the person actually posted or said, and you reference it.
2. ✅ You say who you are and that you are connected to Kixly.
3. ✅ It is written for that one person — you could not paste it to 50 others.
4. ✅ It offers something useful (a walkthrough, a guide, an honest answer), not just a link.
5. ✅ It respects the community's rules.
6. ✅ It is easy to say no to, and says we will stop if they want.
7. ✅ No guaranteed profits, no "sure thing", no pressure.
8. ✅ It is logged in the tracker with the source and the exact message.

**It is spam if any of these are true:**

- ❌ Sent to someone who never posted anything relevant.
- ❌ The same text sent to many people.
- ❌ Sent with a fake identity or pretending to be "just a user".
- ❌ Contains a link with no context or no disclosure.
- ❌ Sent after the person said no or did not reply twice.
- ❌ Promises income, profit, or "safe" bets.
- ❌ Sent by a bot or in bulk.
- ❌ Ignores the community's own rules.

### 4.8 How we evaluate shiller performance (quality over volume)

Paying per message would destroy the whole plan — people would spam. So we score **quality**, and the volume target is a **cap, not a target to beat**.

| Respects community rules | 2 |

| Positive-reply rate | 20% | Above team average |

**Automatic removal triggers:**
- Any fake identity or fake testimonial.
- Any ban caused by ignoring community rules.

## 5. Korean-Language Outreach Strategy

### 5.1 Why this needs its own section

Korean is not just "English translated". Korean online communities have their own etiquette, their own slang, and — importantly — strict rules about promotion. A word-for-word translation will look foreign, and foreign-looking messages get ignored or reported.

### 5.2 How we identify Korean-speaking prospects

| Step | How |
|---|---|
| 1. Find the rooms | Search Naver Cafe, Naver Band, KakaoTalk open chat, DC Inside, FM Korea, Clien, Ruliweb, Inven, and Korean-language Telegram/Discord directories using the Korean keywords in section 3.2 |
| 2. Check the language | Read the last 50 messages. Is it mostly Korean? Is there mixed English crypto slang? |
| 3. Check the topic | Are they talking about KBO, 해외축구 (overseas football), LoL/Valorant/StarCraft, or prediction markets? |
| 4. Check the rules | Most Korean rooms post rules in a pinned message or a 공지 (notice). Read it. If promotion is banned, we contact the admin only. |
| 5. Check activity | Messages in the last 48 hours? Real people, or bots? |
| 6. Score the person | Did they post a prediction with reasoning? Did they ask about odds? Did they use prediction-market vocabulary? |
| 7. Log before contacting | Enter them in `Master Prospects` with language = KO and source = platform + room type |

### 5.3 The Korean-speaking shiller / translator

| Option | How | Cost | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hire a Korean-speaking shiller directly | Same hiring process as section 4.4, with a Korean writing test | Rev share + bonus | Best option — one person owns the segment |
| Pair a Korean translator with an English shiller | Translator reviews and rewrites every message | Small fixed fee per review | Good backup |
| Hire a freelance Korean reviewer only | Reviews scripts and a sample of sent messages | Small fixed fee | Minimum acceptable |

**Rules for the Korean role:**

- All Korean scripts must be written or reviewed by a fluent speaker. Machine translation alone is **not** allowed for anything we send.
- Machine translation is allowed for *reading* (understanding what someone posted), not for *writing*.
- The Korean shiller logs in Korean **and** a one-line English summary, so the BD Lead and management can read it.
- Day 1: the Korean shiller rewrites all Korean scripts in their own words. That version becomes the approved script.

### 5.4 Localising naturally, not word-for-word

| English idea | Bad literal Korean | Natural Korean |
|---|---|---|
| "Hey friend!" | "안녕 친구!" (sounds odd) | "안녕하세요!" (normal polite greeting) |
| "Check out this platform" | "이 플랫폼을 확인하세요" (stiff) | "한번 둘러보셔도 좋을 것 같아요" (softer, natural) |
| "Make money" | "돈을 버세요" (pushy) | ❌ Do not use at all — no earnings language |
| "I think you'll like it" | "당신이 좋아할 것 같아요" | "관심 있으실 것 같아서 연락드렸어요" |

**Simple rules we give the Korean shiller:**

1. Use polite 요-style (해요체/합니다체). Never 반말 (casual speech) to strangers.
2. Apologise for interrupting — this is normal and expected in Korean online culture.
3. Be indirect. "부담스러우시면 그냥 넘어가셔도 괜찮습니다" (if it's a burden, just ignore this) works better than a hard ask.
4. Disclose early. Koreans are very sensitive to hidden advertising (뒷광고). Say upfront that you are contacting on behalf of a platform.
5. Short is better. Two or three sentences for the first message.

### 5.5 Common Korean questions and objections

| Korean question | Meaning | How to answer (short version) |
|---|---|---|
| "이거 도박 아니에요?" | "Isn't this gambling?" | Explain it is a prediction market where prices move with the crowd; no guaranteed returns; only for eligible users |
| "USDT는 어떻게 구해요?" | "How do I get USDT?" | Point to the official Kixly guide only. Never give financial advice or tell them where to buy |
| "수익 보장돼요?" | "Are profits guaranteed?" | **No.** Never. Say clearly that losses are possible |
| "한국에서 이용 가능해요?" | "Can I use it in Korea?" | Do not guess. Point to Kixly's official terms/eligibility page and let them check |
| "뒷광고 아니에요?" | "Isn't this hidden advertising?" | Say plainly: yes, this is promotional, and I may be compensated |
| "링크 안 들어가요" | "I don't open links" | Offer the guide in text form first. If they still refuse, stop |
| "개인정보 왜 필요해요?" | "Why do you need my info?" | Explain only what Kixly requires; never ask for extra personal data |

**Never do these in Korean outreach:**

- Never promise 수익 (profit), 확정 (certainty), or 원금 보장 (capital protection).
- Never say "한국에서 합법입니다" (it's legal in Korea). We do not know that. Point to official terms.
- Never ask for a user's real name, phone number, ID, or wallet keys.

### 6.2 Lead status system

Every prospect has exactly one status at a time. This is the backbone of the whole tracker.

| # | Status | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **New Prospect** | Found and logged, not yet checked |
| 2 | **Qualified** | Passed the relevance check, ready to contact |
| 3 | **Contacted** | Initial message sent |
| 4 | **Replied** | They answered (any answer, including negative) |
| 5 | **Interested** | They asked a question or agreed to look |
| 6 | **Link Clicked** | Tracking shows a click on their link |
| 7 | **Signed Up** | Account created, attributed to the link |
| 8 | **Verified Deposit** | First-time deposit, confirmed in platform reporting |
| 9 | **First Bet** | Placed at least one bet |
| 10 | **Repeat Activity** | Bet or deposit again on a later day |
| — | **Follow-up Required** | No reply yet, follow-up is due |
| — | **No Reply** | No answer after 1 follow-up. **Stop.** |
| — | **Not Interested** | Said no. **Stop permanently.** |
| — | **Ineligible** | Underage, restricted region, bot, or not a real person. **Stop.** |

**Movement rules:**

- A prospect can only move forward or into a stop status. No moving backwards.
- `Not Interested`, `No Reply` and `Ineligible` are **final**. The person goes on the team-wide `Do Not Contact` list.
- `Verified Deposit` is the only status that counts for bonuses. Never `Signed Up`.
- If a deposit is later refunded, charged back, or found to be a shiller's own money, it is reversed and the bonus is cancelled.

### 6.3 How we avoid duplicate outreach

This is a real risk when 5 people work the same communities. Four rules:

1. **Community ownership.** Each shiller is assigned specific communities. Nobody DMs in a community they do not own, unless they claim it first.
2. **Claim before contact.** You cannot message someone until you have set `Owner` and `Claimed at` in `Master Prospects`. First claim wins.
3. **Unique prospect ID.** Format: `platform-roomtype-username` (e.g. `discord-predictmarket-user123`). The sheet blocks duplicates on this column.
4. **Cross-check at the huddle.** Each morning, the BD Lead runs a duplicate check on the previous day's rows and reads out any clash.

**What happens on a clash:** the second shiller drops the prospect, marks it `Duplicate`, and picks a new one. No exceptions, no arguments.

## 8. Decision Framework: Day 7, Day 14, Day 21

### 8.1 Day 7 checkpoint

By Day 7 we will have sent roughly **110–130 initial messages** [ASSUMPTION]. That is enough to judge messages, not enough to judge deposits.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Messages sent vs plan | 90%+ | 70–89% | <70% | Find out why. Recruitment or quality problem? |
| Reply rate (pooled) | ≥15% | 8–14% | <8% | Rewrite openers |
| Positive replies | ≥8 | 3–7 | <3 | Wrong audience — change communities |
| Link clicks | ≥6 | 2–5 | <2 | Link or timing problem — test in-message vs after-interest |
| Signups | ≥2 | 1 | 0 | Watch closely; not yet fatal |
| Bans / complaints | 0 | 0 | ≥1 | Immediate review. Pause the shiller involved |
| Tracker accuracy | 100% | 90–99% | <90% | Retrain before Day 8 |

1. The winning opener per segment (kill the loser).
2. Updated community assignments.
3. A written note on the top 3 objections heard.
4. QA scores for every shiller. Any shiller below 7/10 average gets retrained or replaced.

|---|---|

#### Option 1 — STOP or REDESIGN

Choose this if **any** of these are true:

| Trigger | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Fewer than 124 of 310 planned messages sent, without a good reason | The team cannot execute the model |
| Reply rate below 5% after 150+ contacts | The audience does not want to talk to us |
| Zero positive replies after 200+ contacts | The offer or the approach is wrong |
| Tracking cannot attribute signups to shillers, and is not fixed within 3 days | We cannot measure anything, so continuing is wasted effort |
| Any community ban, or 2+ complaints from prospects | Brand damage is worse than the upside |
| Korean legal/compliance review comes back negative | We must not proceed in that market |

**What "stop" means:** end the DM pilot. Keep the tracker, keep the scripts, keep the learnings. Move the remaining effort to admin partnerships and content (section 12), which are cheaper per contact and lower risk.

#### Option 2 — EXTEND TO DAY 21

Choose this only if **all** of these are true:

| Condition | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Reply rate is healthy | ≥15% on at least 200 contacts |
| There is real interest | ≥8 positive replies |
| Clicks are happening | ≥8 link clicks |
| Deposits are inconclusive, not absent | 0–2 verified deposits |
| Tracking works | Proven end-to-end |
| No bans or unresolved complaints | Zero |
| There is a specific, named thing to fix | e.g. "signups are fine but deposits stall at KYC" |

**An extension is justified when:** we have a clear signal up-funnel (people reply and click) but too few people have reached the bottom to judge. More contacts will answer the open question.

**An extension is NOT justified when:** we simply missed our target and hope for better luck. Adding 7 days to a campaign with a 4% reply rate just produces more silence. In that case, redesign (Option 1) instead.

#### Option 3 — SCALE GRADUALLY

Choose this only if **all** of these are true:

| Condition | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Verified depositors | ≥3 |
| First bets | ≥2 (depositors who actually bet) |
| Cost per verified depositor | At or below the approved ceiling |
| Tracking | Fully working |
| Compliance | Zero bans, zero complaints, legal sign-off held |
| At least one segment clearly beating the others | Enough to justify reallocation |

**How to scale carefully (not aggressively):** add 2 shillers, not 10. Extend to Day 28 only with a fresh decision, using the same rules.

### 8.3 What must improve during Days 15–21

If we extend, the extension is **not** "more of the same". Each day must have a job.

| Days | Focus | Concrete action |
|---|---|---|
| 15–16 | Fix the weakest funnel stage only | If click→signup is weak, rewrite the landing explanation. If signup→deposit is weak, build a "first deposit help" message |
| 15–17 | Grow the best segment | Move 1 shiller from the weakest segment into the strongest |
| 15–18 | Push admin/partner deals to a decision | Ask every warm admin for a yes/no by Day 18 |
| 17–19 | Deepen the Korean sample | If P2 is promising but small, give it extra volume to reach 100+ contacts |
| 18–20 | Second-order conversion | Re-contact everyone marked `Interested` who never deposited. One message only |
| 20–21 | Clean the data | Verify every deposit against platform reporting. Remove any suspicious entries |
| **21** | **Final decision** | Stop / scale / rebuild. **There is no Day 28** |

### 8.4 Day 21 — firm decision

| Outcome | Condition | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | ≥5 verified depositors, ≥3 first bets, CAC under ceiling | Add shillers in pairs; double down on the winning segment; move budget to admin partnerships |
| **Keep small / maintain** | 2–4 verified depositors, good engagement, CAC near ceiling | Run a smaller always-on team; shift most effort to partnerships and content |
| **Stop the DM channel** | ≤1 verified depositor, or CAC above ceiling | Close the shiller DM programme. Reallocate to community partnerships and content only |

**The rule we commit to now:** we do not extend past Day 21 on the same plan. If the answer is still unclear on Day 21, the answer is "no" — and we redesign from scratch.

### 9.2 Formula definitions

| **Positive share of replies** | Positive replies ÷ total replies | Tells us conversation quality |
| **Click-through rate (CTR)** | Unique link clicks ÷ positive replies | Use unique clicks, not raw clicks |
| **Signup conversion rate** | Signups ÷ unique link clicks | |

| **First-bet rate** | First bets ÷ verified deposits | Catches "deposited but froze" |
| **Repeat bettor rate** | Users with activity on 2+ separate days ÷ first bettors | |

| **Contribution per acquired user** | (Net revenue from that user over N days) − (revenue share + bonus + allocated ops cost) | **Cannot be calculated yet** — needs deposit size, margin and retention data [TO FILL] |

**Honest note:** "zero budget" does not mean "zero cost". People's time is a cost. If shillers work on pure revenue share with no base pay, expect lower commitment and higher drop-out. That is a real trade-off, not a detail.

### 10.2 Three compensation models

| Model | How it works | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Pure revenue share** | Shiller earns a % of net revenue from their users for a set period | $0 upfront; only pay for real value | Slow first payment; weak motivation early; high drop-out | Testing with people who already believe in the product |
| **B. Fixed + performance** | Small daily/weekly stipend + bonus per verified depositor who bets | Reliable commitment; controllable cost; quality-focused | Requires some cash upfront | **Recommended for this pilot** |
| **C. Performance only (CPA)** | One payment per verified depositor who places a first bet | Clean, easy to measure; $0 if nobody converts | Pushes people toward low-quality or self-referred users; needs strong anti-fraud rules | Scaling later, once fraud controls are proven |

**[PROPOSAL] Recommended: Model B (fixed + performance) for the pilot,** with a strict quality gate.

### 10.3 Recommended incentive structure

| Component | Proposed structure | Quality gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Base stipend** | Small fixed amount per active day (days with 5 logged quality messages and a complete daily report) | No report = no stipend for that day |
| **Quality bonus** | Paid weekly if the shiller's average QA score is 8/10 or higher | BD Lead scored, not self-reported |
| **Conversion bonus** | Paid per **verified depositor who places at least one bet** | Not paid for signups. Not paid for deposits that never bet |
| **Retention bonus** | Small extra if that user is still active at day 30 | Only if the data is available |
| **Revenue share** | % of net revenue from their referred users, for a defined period | Only on net revenue, after bonuses and refunds |
| **Clawback** | Any bonus paid on a fraudulent, self-referred, refunded or ineligible user is taken back | Non-negotiable |

**Anti-fraud rules (must be approved before hiring):**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| No self-referrals | Shillers cannot deposit through their own link and claim a bonus |
| No incentivised signups | Shillers cannot pay users to sign up or deposit |
| No shared or bought accounts | One real person per account |
| Ineligible users don't count | Underage, restricted-region, or duplicate users are excluded |
| Suspect deposits are held | Held for 30 days against chargebacks and reversals |
| Random checks | BD Lead spot-verifies 10% of claimed conversions |

### 12.1 Why this track exists

| Step | Action |

| 5 | **Accept "no" quickly.** Thank them, log `Not Interested`, move on. Do not negotiate with an admin who has said no. |

### 12.7 Why partnerships are usually the better long-term bet

1. **One approval reaches many people.** A single pinned post can outperform weeks of DMs.
2. **It does not burn relationships.** Cold DMs annoy people. Approved posts do not.

## 15. Final Recommendations and Immediate Action Items

### 15.1 Recommendations

1. **Run the pilot, but call it what it is: a learning test.** With 310 contacts, expect 0–5 verified depositors. Judge the pilot on reply quality, click behaviour, and tracking reliability — not only on deposit count.
2. **Put most of the effort on competitor users (Priority 1).** They already understand prediction markets, so the conversation is shorter and the conversion path is shorter.
3. **Test Korean properly, but only after legal clearance.** Use a real Korean speaker. Do not machine-translate what we send. Keep the Korean sample separate so we can compare it honestly.
4. **Do not skip the Day-2 soft launch.** Ten messages that prove tracking works are worth more than 300 messages we cannot attribute.
5. **Pay for quality, not volume.** Five capped messages per day per shiller. Bonus on verified depositors who actually bet. Never bonus on messages sent.
6. **Start the admin/partner track on Day 6 at the latest.** It is more sustainable than 1-to-1 DMs: one approved post reaches more qualified people than a week of DMs, and it carries far less ban risk.
7. **Commit to a firm Day-21 decision now.** No automatic extensions. If the answer is unclear at Day 21, the answer is no, and we redesign.
8. **Get the 9 blocking resources in place before Day 1.** Launching without tracking or approved commission terms wastes the whole 14 days.
