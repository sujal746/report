1. Executive Summary#

1.1 The question we are answering#

Can 4–5 coordinated shillers, each sending 5 good personal messages per day, bring us verified depositors and repeat bettors from competitor communities and Korean-language communities within 14–21 days — without spending much money upfront and without spamming anyone?

1.2 Short answer#

Yes, it is worth testing — but we should be realistic about what the test can prove.

Here is the honest maths, using 5 shillers over 14 days:

  • 5 shillers × 5 messages × 12 full outreach days = 310 unique people contacted. PROPOSAL
  • At realistic response rates, that produces roughly 0 to 5 verified first-time depositors over the whole 14 days. [ASSUMPTION — see section 9.4]

That is a small number. It is enough to tell us which messages and which audiences get replies, and it gives us a first signal on deposits. It is not enough to prove that deposits will scale.

So we recommend treating this as a learning pilot, not a volume acquisition channel. The real goal for 14 days is:

  1. Find out which audience replies and clicks at all.
  2. Find out whether Korean-language outreach works better or worse than competitor-community outreach.
  3. Build a tracking system that can prove where every depositor came from.
  4. Build a small, trained team we can scale later if the signal is good.

2. Objective and Key Hypotheses#

2.1 Objective#

Acquire genuine, relevant bettors — people who understand sports prediction and might actually use Kixly — at minimal upfront cost, and prove that the channel works before spending money on it.

PROPOSAL Primary success metric: verified first-time depositors who go on to place at least one bet.

PROPOSAL Secondary metrics: reply rate, click rate, signup rate, first-bet rate, repeat activity, cost per verified depositor.

A hypothesis is just a "guess we want to check". We are testing five.

2.3 What we are not doing#

Writing this down so nobody is confused later:

  • ❌ No scraping of private member lists.
  • ❌ No mass unsolicited bulk messaging or bots.
  • ❌ No fake identities, fake accounts or fake "I'm just a normal user" stories.
  • ❌ No fabricated testimonials, screenshots or earnings claims.
  • ❌ No guaranteed-profit or "sure win" language.
  • ❌ No contacting anyone who already said no.
  • ❌ No contacting minors or people in restricted regions.
  • ❌ No paying shillers per message sent.

4.6 Onboarding (Day 1, about 3 hours)#

  1. Briefing (45 min): what Kixly is, what we are testing, what is forbidden.
  2. Product walkthrough (30 min): how to sign up, deposit, place a bet, withdraw. Every shiller must do this themselves with a tiny amount so they can answer basic questions. [PROPOSAL — needs approval for test funds]
  3. Rules session (30 min): community rules, disclosure rules, no-spam rules, stop rules.
  4. Tracker training (30 min): how to claim a prospect, how to log, how to fill the daily report.
  5. Practice (45 min): each shiller writes 5 messages. BD Lead reviews and approves or rejects each one.
  6. Sign-off: shiller signs the outreach & disclosure guidelines (section 11, item 10).

4.7 High-quality DM vs spam — the checklist#

A message is high quality only if it passes all 8:

  1. ✅ You read something the person actually posted or said, and you reference it.
  2. ✅ You say who you are and that you are connected to Kixly.
  3. ✅ It is written for that one person — you could not paste it to 50 others.
  4. ✅ It offers something useful (a walkthrough, a guide, an honest answer), not just a link.
  5. ✅ It respects the community's rules.
  6. ✅ It is easy to say no to, and says we will stop if they want.
  7. ✅ No guaranteed profits, no "sure thing", no pressure.
  8. ✅ It is logged in the tracker with the source and the exact message.

It is spam if any of these are true:

  • ❌ Sent to someone who never posted anything relevant.
  • ❌ The same text sent to many people.
  • ❌ Sent with a fake identity or pretending to be "just a user".
  • ❌ Contains a link with no context or no disclosure.
  • ❌ Sent after the person said no or did not reply twice.
  • ❌ Promises income, profit, or "safe" bets.
  • ❌ Sent by a bot or in bulk.
  • ❌ Ignores the community's own rules.

5. Korean-Language Outreach Strategy#

5.1 Why this needs its own section#

Korean is not just "English translated". Korean online communities have their own etiquette, their own slang, and — importantly — strict rules about promotion. A word-for-word translation will look foreign, and foreign-looking messages get ignored or reported.

5.2 How we identify Korean-speaking prospects#

Step How
1. Find the rooms Search Naver Cafe, Naver Band, KakaoTalk open chat, DC Inside, FM Korea, Clien, Ruliweb, Inven, and Korean-language Telegram/Discord directories using the Korean keywords in section 3.2
2. Check the language Read the last 50 messages. Is it mostly Korean? Is there mixed English crypto slang?
3. Check the topic Are they talking about KBO, 해외축구 (overseas football), LoL/Valorant/StarCraft, or prediction markets?
4. Check the rules Most Korean rooms post rules in a pinned message or a 공지 (notice). Read it. If promotion is banned, we contact the admin only.
5. Check activity Messages in the last 48 hours? Real people, or bots?
6. Score the person Did they post a prediction with reasoning? Did they ask about odds? Did they use prediction-market vocabulary?
7. Log before contacting Enter them in Master Prospects with language = KO and source = platform + room type

5.3 The Korean-speaking shiller / translator#

Option How Cost When to use
Hire a Korean-speaking shiller directly Same hiring process as section 4.4, with a Korean writing test Rev share + bonus Best option — one person owns the segment
Pair a Korean translator with an English shiller Translator reviews and rewrites every message Small fixed fee per review Good backup
Hire a freelance Korean reviewer only Reviews scripts and a sample of sent messages Small fixed fee Minimum acceptable

Rules for the Korean role:

  • All Korean scripts must be written or reviewed by a fluent speaker. Machine translation alone is not allowed for anything we send.
  • Machine translation is allowed for reading (understanding what someone posted), not for writing.
  • The Korean shiller logs in Korean and a one-line English summary, so the BD Lead and management can read it.
  • Day 1: the Korean shiller rewrites all Korean scripts in their own words. That version becomes the approved script.

5.4 Localising naturally, not word-for-word#

English idea Bad literal Korean Natural Korean
"Hey friend!" "안녕 친구!" (sounds odd) "안녕하세요!" (normal polite greeting)
"Check out this platform" "이 플랫폼을 확인하세요" (stiff) "한번 둘러보셔도 좋을 것 같아요" (softer, natural)
"Make money" "돈을 버세요" (pushy) ❌ Do not use at all — no earnings language
"I think you'll like it" "당신이 좋아할 것 같아요" "관심 있으실 것 같아서 연락드렸어요"

Simple rules we give the Korean shiller:

  1. Use polite 요-style (해요체/합니다체). Never 반말 (casual speech) to strangers.
  2. Apologise for interrupting — this is normal and expected in Korean online culture.
  3. Be indirect. "부담스러우시면 그냥 넘어가셔도 괜찮습니다" (if it's a burden, just ignore this) works better than a hard ask.
  4. Disclose early. Koreans are very sensitive to hidden advertising (뒷광고). Say upfront that you are contacting on behalf of a platform.
  5. Short is better. Two or three sentences for the first message.

6.3 How we avoid duplicate outreach#

This is a real risk when 5 people work the same communities. Four rules:

  1. Community ownership. Each shiller is assigned specific communities. Nobody DMs in a community they do not own, unless they claim it first.
  2. Claim before contact. You cannot message someone until you have set Owner and Claimed at in Master Prospects. First claim wins.
  3. Unique prospect ID. Format: platform-roomtype-username (e.g. discord-predictmarket-user123). The sheet blocks duplicates on this column.
  4. Cross-check at the huddle. Each morning, the BD Lead runs a duplicate check on the previous day's rows and reads out any clash.

What happens on a clash: the second shiller drops the prospect, marks it Duplicate, and picks a new one. No exceptions, no arguments.
